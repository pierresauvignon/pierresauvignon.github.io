The backlash gets a vote, and Jensen has a very bad week

Pierre Sauvignon · 2 min read

Americans dislike data centres about equally whichever way they vote, but it was Democrats who ran on it, and they swept. Forty of 41 local ballot measures went against data centres, and the new governors of Texas, Georgia and Ohio all promised to slow construction, which by one count covers 41% of the planned US pipeline. Texas alone is a quarter of that, and its new governor is talking about projects without permits, not the ones already being built, so I doubt it slows much. What voters are actually angry about is water and power bills, which are planning problems. The market still fell on it, having already fallen because these companies spend too much. Elon Musk put about $470m into the election, $200m of it in Texas, and his candidates lost. Meanwhile Anthropic's updated filing shows Q3 revenue of $17bn, up 47% on the quarter after 144% the quarter before. Call it customers managing token costs, or OpenAI taking share back, or just a bigger base. Investors have told the bankers $2tn is gone and the talk is now $1.2tn to $1.3tn.

Then Nvidia. US intelligence reportedly believes Jensen Huang was having an affair with a Megaspeed executive they consider a Chinese asset, at a cloud company already suspected of funnelling Nvidia chips into China. He admitted it on Sunday, the board opened an investigation, and by Thursday he had resigned. The same week Oracle dropped a $7bn deal renting 100,000 GPUs to Tencent after Washington leaned on it, since there's no point banning chip exports if you can rent the chips from Singapore. And it turns out the $6.3bn Binance heist needed no clever cryptography: North Korea ran a fake job interview with an engineer for weeks until he installed a coding test with malware inside. For anyone excited about agents doing the shopping, Uber did 320 million trips and orders in Halloween week, and AI agents booked 500 meals and four rides. Four. It's early.
